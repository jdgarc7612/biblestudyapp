## Product Vision

DeepWord: Bible Study Companion is built to feel like a trusted daily companion for
Scripture — not a utility, not a reference tool. Every feature should serve one of two
core users:

1. **New/casual believers** who have real questions about the Bible and want a low-friction
   way to start a daily habit of reading and asking questions without judgment.
2. **Longtime, serious Christians** who want tools that go deeper — cross-references,
   study aids, memorization — not just surface-level content.

Both user types should feel the app respects their intelligence and their faith. Nothing
should feel dumbed-down, preachy, or like generic "wellness app" filler.

### The signature feature: AI Q&A / Study Companion
This is the "wow" moment for first-time users. It should feel like:
- Talking to a knowledgeable, warm, patient study partner — never robotic or clinical
- Fast and low-friction (no long forms, no friction before getting an answer)
- Trustworthy: answers should be groundable in Scripture, cite verses where relevant,
  and avoid theological overreach or unsupported claims
- A natural companion to the Read tab — the two should feel connected, not siloed

### Design quality bar
Visual and interaction quality should be benchmarked against YouVersion / Bible App —
warm, polished, editorial, not sterile or clinical. Specifically:
- Generous whitespace, calm color palette (soft blues #4A6FA5, earth tones #C19A6B)
- Typography should feel intentional and readable for long-form Scripture reading
- Motion/transitions should feel smooth and premium, never janky or default-feeling
- Avoid anything that reads as a generic CRUD app or admin dashboard

### Standing instruction for all future work
When implementing any feature, prioritize:
1. Does this feel genuinely valuable, not just functional?
2. Does this match the visual quality bar above?
3. Does this serve either the new-believer or serious-student use case (ideally both)?

If a requested feature is ambiguous or could be built at a "basic" or "great" level,
default to asking or proposing the more polished version rather than the minimal one.
